Our vision

We want to understand aging well enough to extend healthy human lifespan by an order of magnitude.

Evolution has already done more than that. A mouse lives 4 years and a bowhead whale more than 200. Current interventions change lifespan by a small fraction. The larger differences probably depend on mechanisms they do not reach.

We also ask what aging is. Gerophysics treats the organism as a physical system that keeps itself stable. Aging is the loss of that stability. The theory tells the models what to look for.

The lab is computational and experimental. Models and agents propose interventions. Experiments decide. The results train the next model.

Research

Most AI for biology works on proteins and cells. Aging shows itself in the whole organism and in the lifespan of a species. We model all six scales. Much of our work starts at the species and the human, where few models exist.

Every project runs one loop. We measure a biological state and model it. The model nominates an intervention. We design the molecule and test it. The result goes back into the model.

Our first system

Brain resilience

Aging is the largest risk factor for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease. Yet some people keep their memory into old age even when their brains carry the pathology. We want to know what protects them.

We expect the longest-lived species to have the most resilient brains. A bowhead whale can live more than two centuries. A Greenland shark may live four. A naked mole-rat outlives other rodents of its size by decades. We compare their brains with human brains to find protective molecules and cell states.

In humans we model brain aging from molecular profiles of brain, blood and spinal fluid. Resilience then becomes measurable. It is how far a person stays ahead of the decline their age and pathology predict. What explains that gap becomes a target.

How we work

AI agents that read data and papers

Public repositories hold millions of molecular profiles. Most were analyzed once, for the question of the study that made them. Our agents read the data and the papers together and propose what to test. Every claim keeps a record of the data and code behind it.

In ClockBase Agent the agents reanalyzed public profiles with aging clocks. They nominated interventions predicted to lower biological age. One of them, ouabain, reduced frailty in aged mice.

Directions

Six areas where the first projects start

Gerophysics

Aging from first principles. An organism holds its state stable through constant repair. Aging is the slow drift of that state towards the point where stability is lost.

  • Loss of resilience: slower recovery and larger fluctuations with age
  • Scaling laws that link lifespan to body size across species
  • Mathematical models of aging checked against data from many species

Foundation and world models of biology

A world model predicts how a biological state changes over time and in response to an intervention.

  • Foundation models of molecular state across omics
  • World models of the aging cell
  • Pan-species and evolutionary models of longevity

Measuring and modeling aging

  • Biological age and module-specific aging signatures
  • Aging and resilience trajectories
  • Causal aging biomarkers

AI agents for scientific discovery

  • Autonomous discovery of interventions from public data
  • Agent workflows in which every step can be inspected
  • Synthesis of public datasets and the literature

Generative therapeutic design

  • De novo protein design for protein aggregation and neurodegeneration
  • Evolution-informed protein engineering
  • Experimental validation of targets proposed by the models

Disease areas

  • Brain resilience and neurodegeneration
  • Cancer and other diseases of aging

    Kejun Albert Ying, PhD

    Principal investigator. Incoming Assistant Professor.
