Our vision

We want to understand aging well enough to extend healthy human lifespan by an order of magnitude.

Evolution has already done more than that. Maximum lifespan runs from 4 years in a mouse to more than 200 in a bowhead whale. The best interventions extend mouse lifespan by a small fraction. The differences between species likely depend on mechanisms these interventions do not reach.

We also ask what aging is. Gerophysics studies aging from physical first principles. The theory tells the models what to look for.

The lab is computational and experimental. We test the predictions we act on in cells and animals.

Research

Most AI for biology works on proteins and cells. Aging shows itself in whole organisms, in populations and in the lifespan of a species. We work across six scales. Much of our work starts at the species, the population and the individual. AI for biology has studied these scales less.

Every project runs one loop. We measure a biological state and model it. The model nominates an intervention. We design the molecule and test it. The result goes back into the model.

Research directions

AI agents that read data and papers

Public repositories hold millions of molecular profiles. Most were analyzed once for the question of the study that made them. Our agents read the data and the papers together and propose what to test. Each result records the data, code and papers it came from.

In ClockBase Agent the agents reanalyzed public profiles with aging clocks. They nominated interventions predicted to lower biological age. One of them, ouabain, slowed frailty progression and reduced neuroinflammation in aged mice.

Brain resilience

Aging is the largest risk factor for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease. Yet some people keep their memory into old age even when their brains carry the pathology. We want to know what protects them.

We expect the longest-lived species to have the most resilient brains. A bowhead whale can live an estimated two centuries. A Greenland shark may live four. A naked mole-rat outlives other rodents of its size by decades. We compare their brains with human brains to find protective molecules and cell states.

In people we model brain aging from molecular profiles of brain, blood and spinal fluid. Resilience then becomes measurable. It is how far a person stays ahead of the decline their age and pathology predict. What explains that gap becomes a target. We will apply the same approach to cancer and other diseases of aging.

Foundation and world models of biology

A world model predicts how a biological state changes over time and in response to an intervention.

  • Foundation models of molecular state across omics
  • World models of the aging cell
  • Pan-species and evolutionary models of longevity

Measuring and modeling aging

  • Biological age and module-specific aging signatures
  • Aging and resilience trajectories
  • Causal aging biomarkers

Generative therapeutic design

  • De novo protein design for protein aggregation and neurodegeneration
  • Evolution-informed protein engineering
  • Experimental validation of targets proposed by the models

Gerophysics

An organism holds its state stable through constant repair. Aging is the slow drift of that state towards the point where stability is lost.

  • Loss of resilience with slower recovery and larger fluctuations
  • Scaling laws that link lifespan to body size across species
  • Mathematical models of aging checked against data from many species
